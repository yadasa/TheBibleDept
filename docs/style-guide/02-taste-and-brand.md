# Taste and brand direction

## The shared taste

The design should make serious Bible learning feel within reach. Its authority comes from confident teaching, strong composition, and clear instructions. Its warmth comes from people, ordinary language, and reassurance. Treat the following as recommended judgment rules inferred from the two references.

| Principle | Design decision | Reject when |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Teach with clarity | One promise, a readable explanation, and a specific next action | The visitor must decode an abstract slogan. |
| Let type lead | Large, compact headlines with deliberate line breaks | Decorative art competes with the main idea. |
| Make expertise human | Real teacher photography, natural expressions, concrete teaching context | Stock symbolism replaces the people doing the teaching. |
| Use orange with intent | Emphasize actions, section identity, and selected states | Every card, paragraph, and icon demands equal attention. |
| Give ideas room | Group related content tightly, separate major ideas generously | Borders and shadows are doing the work that spacing should do. |
| Invite people back | Helpful recovery language, a clear route to resume | Streaks or reminders shame someone for missing a day. |

## Choose one direction

**Bible Dept** is the recommended starting point for the current app and a related daily-practice project. Use warm near-white, deep ink, vivid orange, bold grotesk headings, crisp 4px controls, manuscript texture in selected brand moments, and a portrait that feels like an invitation to learn together.

**ARMA** is a useful reference for a course launch, teaching collection, or editorial campaign. Use the darker neutral, red-orange accent, condensed display typography, a small amount of italic serif emphasis, and historically evocative imagery. Keep long explanations in the shared sans-serif body family.

Do not assemble a third identity by mixing ARMA's condensed-plus-serif title treatment, Bible Dept's script utility, several oranges, and legacy app colors in one view. A shared family can have distinct siblings. Choose a parent direction and document intentional departures.

## Composition rules for the next project

1. Give each section one job: promise, explanation, teacher, process, proof, objections, or next action.
2. Establish one dominant element. A large title can dominate; a portrait can dominate; both should serve the same message.
3. Use full-width color fields for changes in topic or energy. Keep the inner text alignment consistent across sections.
4. Pair one filled action with one quieter alternative when both are needed. Two unrelated filled actions dilute the choice.
5. Alternate dense explanation with an image or a short process section. Avoid a whole page of identical cards.
6. Keep everyday tools calm. Reading Scripture should be visually quieter than a launch page.

## Taste calibration

| Choice | Fits the family | Weakens the family |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Hero | Direct benefit, strong type, human or teaching image | Generic worship photo with vague inspiration |
| Surface | Ink, paper, purposeful orange section | Unrelated gradients or glass panels everywhere |
| Detail | Thin rules, modest corners, restrained icons | Puffy controls, elaborate shadows, many badges |
| Imagery | Documentary portrait or meaningful historical reference | Random antiquity collage with no teaching connection |
| Copy | Specific help, plain next step, patient encouragement | Hype, guilt, invented outcomes or urgency |
| Product state | Resume reading, explain errors, save place | Launch-page sales pressure inside the reader |

## Decision order

When two choices conflict, prioritize reading comfort and task clarity, then brand recognition, then visual novelty. A readable action shade can differ from a marketing orange. A native app can use platform typography while keeping the brand's weight, hierarchy, spacing, and warmth.

Carry the underlying structure forward: confident title, generous separation, purposeful image, clear action, and a teacher's voice. Copying a screenshot's fixed dimensions is less useful than preserving why the composition works.
